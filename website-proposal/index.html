Why now

The website is where a clinic decides whether to answer us.

When a clinic owner gets one of our emails, the first thing they do is look us up. Our outreach emails link to the site, and our Google ad sends people straight to it. What they find there either backs up what we told them, or makes them doubt it.

Most of them will see it on a phone. From September 22 to October 12, 8 of every 10 clicks on our Google ad came from a phone.

The good news: the structure, the colors and a lot of the writing are good, and we keep them. What's hurting us are specific, fixable things: small errors that make us look careless, our biggest strengths not being said at all, and a site that never names the specialties we work with.

What works

What we keep

  • The structure of the home page and the color palette.
  • The writing that speaks to the clinic's pain: "Tired of chasing payments?" and "You're not the only one losing sleep over unpaid claims."
  • "You always get paid first. No hold-ups. No funny business."
  • "Woman-owned, US-based business."
  • Speed. Darrell brought the load time on a phone down from about 5.5 to about 2 seconds.
What we found

What a clinic owner sees today

None of these is big on its own. Together, they make a company that handles other people's money look careless, and that's the one thing a billing company can't look like.

Our main advantage isn't said anywhere

Nowhere does the site say that we only get paid when the clinic gets paid. The closest line is "we invoice you afterward", which can be read the opposite way. It's the reason a clinic would choose us over anyone else, and a visitor never reads it.

Home and both ad pages

The headline promises numbers we can't back up

The first line a visitor reads is "We help therapists get paid up to 50% faster", and further down the home says our system "saves you hours every week". We don't have a source for either one. The 97% also appears with two different names a few lines apart: "claim success rate" and "claim approval rate".

Home and both ad pages

A Gmail address on our own website

The footer shows jcbilling@gmail.com, while the contact form already goes to support@jcbilling.com. A clinic notices a Gmail address right away, and it makes us look like a one-person operation.

Footer of Home, About, Services and Contact

The same problem card appears twice

"No time to follow up" is shown twice, word for word, in the "Sound familiar?" section.

Home and both ad pages

The same testimonial appears twice

The testimonial carousel has three spots, and two of them are S. Rhodes. The testimonials are signed only with initials ("Dr. L. M.", "S. Rhodes"), with no clinic name, so they can look made up even though they're real.

Home and both ad pages

Small writing errors

What other billing companies show that we don't

I went through the sites of companies that rank above us (Park Medical Billing, 247 Medical Billing Services and Therapeutic Billing Co.) on October 12. These are things they have and we could add:

  • A real calendar to book the call. Therapeutic Billing has one right on its page, and Park asks you to pick a day. Today our thank-you page promises a scheduling link that never arrives. We already have the calendar: your Zoom scheduler, the same one our emails use. On the new site, every "Book a call" button opens it, and a clinic that sends the form goes straight to it to pick a time, instead of waiting for someone to answer by email. You can try it in the website preview.
  • How getting started works, step by step, with how long each step takes (Park and 247).
  • The EHR systems they work with. Park names WebPT, AdvancedMD and ChiroTouch. A clinic wants to know right away if it has to change its system.
  • Reports for the client and a named account manager (Park and 247), so the clinic knows who handles its claims. We already send monthly reports (collection rate, denials, unpaid balances and how each insurer pays), so this is something to say, not to add.
  • How they protect patient data (247 explains HIPAA and the BAA). We'd only write what's true for JCB.
  • A note on the contact form asking not to include patient information (Therapeutic Billing).

I'd also add a "Call" button that stays visible on phones on every page, our office hours, and two questions on the form: which service they need and how they heard about us. Until Analytics is connected, that second question is our only way to know what's working.

What I left out on purpose

I didn't use any number or credential we can't back up. In our field, a claim we can't prove is a legal risk, not just a marketing one. That's why the preview doesn't say "up to 40% fewer errors", "5–10 staff hours a week" or "50% faster", and why it shows empty spaces for testimonials and certifications until JCB gives me the real ones. "50% faster" and "saves you hours every week" are on the live site today, so they're part of decision 3.

Your decisions

What I need you and Jennita to decide

  1. Do we publish our full pricing on the site?

    When we did our competitor benchmark, none of the companies in it published their full fee structure upfront, so this would set us apart. Of the companies that rank above us on Google, Therapeutic Billing does publish its prices: a flat monthly fee ($800 for one provider, from $1,500 for small groups), paid whether the claims get paid or not. Nobody publishes a fee that's charged only per paid claim, which makes our model easy to compare. It also matches the commitment "you should understand what you're paying and why before committing".

    My recommendation: yes, with the prices you already confirmed ($2,000 setup, $500/month maintenance, $7 per paid claim or 5% of payments, $500 per entity for credentialing).

  2. Which founding year do we use?

    The About page says JCB was founded in 2021. The Partnership Introduction says "Billing since 2016". And in May JCB confirmed August 2020, which is what our sales materials say. The site needs one clear sentence, for example "Founded in 2020. Billing since 2016."

    Your two call guides still say 2021. I'll update them with whatever you decide.

  3. Where do "97% claim approval rate" and "over 1,000 payouts a month" come from?

    The same question goes for "up to 50% faster" and "saves you hours every week", which are on the live site today. If we can't find where they come from, I'd remove them in Step 1.

    The 97% and the 1,000+ are strong numbers, so in the new home they have their own section, right below the top of the page, with a line under each one saying how it was measured.

    Could you confirm where they come from? How they were measured, over what period, and whether the 97% counts claims approved on the first submission or after corrections. It would help me understand them clearly, and if we can say how we got them (for example, "based on every claim we submitted in 2025"), a clinic is much more likely to believe them.

  4. The Medicare and Medicaid logos and the "HIPAA COMPLIANT" seal

    They sit under "Why therapists trust us", where certifications usually go. I'm not sure the government logos can be used that way, since they could read as an endorsement, and as far as I know HIPAA has no official certification. I'm not a legal source on this, so I'd ask someone who knows before we keep or change them.

  5. Is the first call with you?

    The preview says a clinic's first conversation is twenty minutes with you, as COO. If it should be someone else, I'll change it.

  6. Do we show the technology we use?

    Billing companies that rank above us on Google use their systems as a selling point. Park Medical Billing presents its "SPHERE Revenue Assurance System" and a client login. Integrity Billing, which works in behavioral health, presents "i-bot", its platform for benefit verification and reporting. Clinics want to know how easy the process is: how documentation, signatures, visit logs and patient information reach us.

    We already have a system like that working every day. Showing it would let us compete on equal terms. I know Jennita preferred not to show it before, so I'd like to ask again now that we're working on the content. The Services page already mentions one tool today: "We have partnered with EMRFlow to offer a custom EHR system".

    If the answer is yes, I'd like to ask a few questions to understand how the app works before writing anything about it.

  7. Do we show the people behind JCB?

    Stanford University's guidelines for web credibility, based on research with more than 4,500 people, recommend to "show that honest and trustworthy people stand behind your site" and to highlight the expertise of the team. Nielsen Norman Group, one of the leading research firms on how people use websites, found in its eye-tracking studies that "users ignore stock photos of generic people", while they look closely at photos of "real people who actually work at the company".

    Our clients trust us with their income, so they want to know who is handling it. My proposal is to show the team that leads JCB, with real photos, each person's role, years of experience and what they're in charge of. Who appears is up to you and Jennita.

    If you'd rather not show the team, we keep using stock images, but more natural ones that don't look staged.

  8. Contract, trial period and start time: one answer for the site

    Our sales materials give clinics different answers. Some say the contract is month-to-month, others a one-year agreement, renewable. Some say "No trial periods", others offer an initial period of 60 or 90 days. Getting started takes two to four weeks in some and three to four in others.

    These are some of the first questions a clinic asks, so the pricing page and the FAQ need one answer for each. I'll also update our sales materials so everything says the same thing.
