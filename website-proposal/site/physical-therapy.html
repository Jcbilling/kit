Physical therapy

Billing for physical therapy practices.

Timed units, therapy modifiers, plans of care and the yearly Medicare threshold. Here is what gets physical therapy claims denied, and how to avoid it.

Common billing errors

What gets physical therapy claims denied.

Counting timed units wrong

Units come from the total minutes of all timed services that day, not from each code on its own. Billing more units than the minutes allow means paying money back.

How to avoid itWrite down the minutes of each timed service and add them up before choosing the units.

Documentation that doesn’t show why the care is needed

It’s the main reason Medicare takes money back from private PT practices: 88.6% of improper payments in 2024.

How to avoid itEvery note should connect the treatment to the plan of care, the goals and the patient’s progress.

The plan of care isn’t certified on time

The physician or NPP has to sign the plan within 30 days of the first visit, and it has to be recertified at least every 90 days.

How to avoid itTrack the dates for every patient, and follow up on signatures before they’re late.

No progress report

Medicare requires a progress report at least once every 10 treatment days.

How to avoid itCount treatment days, not calendar weeks.

Missing KX after the threshold

After $2,480 in 2026, a claim without KX is denied.

How to avoid itTrack each patient’s yearly total, including speech therapy they received elsewhere.

Assistant visits without CQ

When an assistant provides the care, the claim needs CQ. It changes the payment, so it’s checked.

How to avoid itMark who provided each service in the note.

Before a claim goes out

A quick check for every physical therapy claim.

  • GP on every line
  • Units match the minutes documented
  • Plan of care signed and current
  • Progress report within the last 10 treatment days
  • KX if the patient passed $2,480
  • CQ if an assistant provided care
